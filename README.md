# Eden Engine

**Eden Engine** began as a simple experiment where a plant would grow whenever a key was pressed. Over time, the project evolved into a much more ambitious ecosystem simulation focused on plant growth, competition, genetics, and environmental interaction.

The goal of the project is to simulate a living garden where every plant behaves differently, competes for resources, and develops according to its own genetic traits. Considerable effort went into making the simulation feel organic, dynamic, and visually engaging.

---

## What is Eden Engine?

Eden Engine is a browser-based ecosystem simulation where users can create and observe a procedurally generated plant ecosystem.

Players can plant seeds directly into the environment and watch them germinate, grow, compete, reproduce, and eventually die based on environmental conditions.

The simulation models several biological and environmental factors including:

* Germination and growth
* Water consumption
* Sunlight competition
* Photosynthesis
* Energy storage
* Genetic variation
* Natural selection concepts
* Day and night cycles
* Environmental effects

Plants grow unique structures, develop branches, produce leaves, and interact with neighboring plants. Taller plants can block sunlight from smaller plants, creating natural competition similar to real ecosystems.

---

## DNA System

One of the core features of Eden Engine is its procedural DNA system.

Every newly planted seed receives a unique genetic sequence that determines its characteristics.

### DNA Traits

Each plant's DNA influences:

* Growth rate
* Branch generation chance
* Leaf size
* Flower color (hue)
* Structural strength
* Maximum height

This creates biodiversity within the simulation, ensuring that no two plants are exactly alike.

### Genetic Inheritance

The project also experiments with inheritance mechanics.

When plants drop seeds, offspring can inherit characteristics from their parent plants. Over time this allows populations to gradually evolve and adapt, creating emergent ecosystem behavior.
